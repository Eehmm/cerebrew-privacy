Cerebrew Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 October 2026

Cerebrew ("the app") is a brain-games app for Android made by Henrik Rømer ("I", "me"). This policy explains what happens to your information when you use the app. In short: your game data stays on your device, and the app shows ads from Google AdMob, which collects some device data to serve them.

Information the app stores on your device

To work, the app stores the following on your device only:

  • Your settings: session length, focus areas, games you have turned off, difficulty mode, sound and vibration preferences.
  • Your game history: which games you played on which days, the difficulty level, your scores and how long each game took.
  • Today's planned session, and the date an end-of-session ad was last shown.

The app does not send this information anywhere, and I have no access to it. There are no accounts, and the app does not ask for your name, email address, contacts, photos or precise location.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app is free and shows ads provided by Google AdMob: a banner on the main screens and at most one full-screen ad a day, after you finish your daily session. Ads never appear during a game.

To serve and measure ads and to prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK in the app automatically collects and shares the following with Google:

  • Device identifiers: your Android advertising ID and app set ID.
  • IP address, which may be used to estimate your general location (such as your country or city).
  • Ad interactions: for example app launches, ad views and taps.
  • Diagnostic information: for example app and SDK performance, launch time and energy use.

This data is encrypted in transit. Google uses it as described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

Your choices

  • Consent (EEA, UK, Switzerland and some US states): when the app first starts, it asks whether you agree to personalised ads, using Google's consent tool. You can change your choice at any time with Settings → Privacy choices. If you don't consent, you may still see non-personalised or limited ads.
  • Advertising ID: you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your device's settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads on most phones).

Android backup

If you have turned on Android's backup feature, Android may include the app's settings and game history in your device backup to your Google account, so they can be restored on a new phone. Google handles that backup under the Google Privacy Policy. I have no access to it. You can turn off backup in your device settings.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to load ads. The games themselves work offline.
  • Advertising ID (and the related Android ad-services permissions): used by AdMob as described above.
  • Vibrate: optional haptic feedback during games. You can turn it off with Settings → Vibrate on answers.

Deleting your data

You can erase your scores, levels and streaks at any time with Settings → Reset progress. Uninstalling the app deletes all of its data from your device. For data held by Google for advertising, use your Google account settings or reset your advertising ID.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13. I do not knowingly collect information from children, and ads in the app are not intended for children.
